Task 9: a child's whole year, and the school's decision about it

Everything else in `results` is reckoned per term. This is the two lines at the
bottom of the card that are not — the average of the session, and the promotion
— and the second of them is the first thing in this app that a machine must not
decide.

**A missing term renormalises, and is never a zero.** The whole module turns on
this. A child who transferred in at second term did not score nothing in first
term; they were not there. Scored as a zero the absence does not merely lower an
average, it propagates into a recommendation: at 20/20/60 a child who sat two
terms and averaged 77.50 reads 62.00, which is under a seventy pass mark, and
the school is advised to make them repeat the year on the strength of a term
they were not enrolled to sit. So the weights of the terms actually sat are
renormalised to sum to a hundred — 20/60 becomes 25/75 — and
`TheTwoTermCaseTests` holds it under both conventions with the numbers written
out, which is what the settled decision asked for.

**Three reasons a term is absent, kept apart.** They renormalise identically and
they are not the same fact: `NOT_ENROLLED` is a transfer, `UNMARKED` is a school
that has not entered its marks, and `NO_TERM` is a session still being taught.
Collapsed into a bare `None` — which was the obvious shape — a marking backlog
looks exactly like a transfer on the one screen a head of year uses to find
both. `TermAbsence` is on the frozen row as well as the live read, and it is
staff-only: a parent reading "no marks were entered" is being shown the school's
filing rather than their child's year.

**The averaging is configurable because the number decides promotions.** Equal
and 20/20/60 are both real, so neither is hardcoded; `EQUAL` is the default,
being what a school that has never thought about it means. A weighting must sum
to a hundred, refused in the service with a sentence naming the total the school
actually typed and again by a check constraint for the import that never reaches
the service. In `EQUAL` mode the weights are **null**, not 33.33/33.33/33.34 —
that is not a straight mean, it is a weighting that favours third term by a
hundredth — and not left at their old values either, because a stale 20/20/60
under a mode that says EQUAL is a field that lies to the next reader.

**The exact mean, and weights that add up.** These pull in opposite directions
and both matter, so the row does both: the stored weights are rounded to two
places and the drift handed to the largest remainders so they total a hundred,
while the average is computed from the exact ratios and rounded once. They
disagree in the last penny by design — 0, 0, 100 stores a third-term weight of
33.34 and an average of 33.33 — and the docstring says so rather than claiming
an auditability the row does not have. When the spare hundredth has nowhere
obvious to go, later terms win it: under an equal weighting every remainder is
identical, so a stable sort would hand it to whichever term was inserted first,
which is a number on a school's screen decided by dictionary order.

**Frozen at third-term release, and third term only.** Alongside the conduct
section and the remarks, inside the same transaction. `sessions.freeze_for_release()`
decides for itself that earlier terms write nothing rather than being called
conditionally from `release()` — a caller that has to remember which freezes
apply to which term will eventually forget. The reason to copy at all is that a
session average reaches backwards through two years of rows a school may
legitimately change: switching from 20/20/60 to a straight mean, revising a
first-term result, correcting a placement in a term long closed. None of those
are misuse and every one rewrites a number a parent is holding.

**Promotion is a suggestion and a decision, and they are different columns.**
The suggestion can only ever be PROMOTED or REPEATED — a pass mark cannot reach
"promoted on trial", which is a judgement about a child who fell short and is
worth carrying anyway. Undecided is the **absence of a row**: there is no
UNDECIDED member and no current-status column, because a status column with a
default is a school-wide promotion performed by a default value, and the audit
would show four hundred children as decided by a principal who reviewed nothing.

**The suggestion is frozen at the moment of the decision, not recomputed.** This
was the question worth arguing about. The gap between suggested and recorded is
the record that a person went against the arithmetic — and the arithmetic is a
function of a weighting the school may change. Recomputed, the same row reads as
agreement or override depending on when it is asked: a school moving from
20/20/60 to a straight mean turns a child who read 47.00 into one who reads
51.67, and a principal who granted a trial pass against a REPEATED suggestion is
retroactively recorded as having demoted a child the system wanted promoted.
There is a test that performs exactly that move and asserts the row does not
budge. The counter-case — a mark that was wrong and got fixed — is task 8's: a
revision makes a new version, so it makes a new decision row.

**Deciding is the principal's, configuring is the office's.** The one place
these two sets deliberately disagree is the administrator, who may set the
school's weighting and may not tell a family their child repeats the year.
`RELEASING_ROLES` already draws that line and task 8 rests on it.

**Found in self-review: a child can collect two frozen session lines.** The
tempting claim is that at most one exists — `ClassPlacement` allows one group
per child per term, so a child is on exactly one third-term roster. True at any
instant, false over time: release JSS 1A, move the child to JSS 3B, release JSS
3B, and both releases freeze them. Both rows are real records of releases that
happened and the table is append-only, so the question is which one is the card.
It is the first, on the rule `0010`, `0011` and issue #27 all turn on — a
released card keeps saying what it said, and a later release cannot reach
backwards into one already in a parent's hand.

**And the test for it was worthless twice before it worked.** The two
behavioural tests pass with the ordering removed, because Postgres with no ORDER
BY hands back a freshly-inserted pair in insertion order — so they were getting
the right row by luck. Verified by running the control rather than assumed. The
captured-SQL test that replaced them was wrong in its own way and passed against
an unordered query for two separate reasons: `QuerySet.first()` adds an ORDER BY
on the primary key when a queryset has none, so asserting an ORDER BY exists
proves nothing; and `created_at` is in every SELECT list, so asserting it
appears matched the projection. It asserts on the ORDER BY clause alone now, and
the control fails as it should. `LockScopeTests` keeps a behavioural pair and a
SQL assertion for the same reason and says so at length; this is that shape.

**`positions._round()` is now `round_percentage()`**, public and unchanged. The
session average has to round the way every other percentage in this app rounds,
and a second `quantize()` in a second module is the divergence `_mean()` was
extracted to end.

Forty-nine tests. Two schools throughout, and the second is used rather than
built and ignored: a weighting is per-school, a pass mark is per-school, and
both are asserted not to cross.

Not in this PR, and named rather than left to be noticed: **the read API**.
`results/api.py` exposes one broadsheet route, and the session view and
promotion sheet are not on it. That PR is where the staff-only rules above have
to be enforced at the serializer — the absence reasons and the suggestion, the
same way position already is.
