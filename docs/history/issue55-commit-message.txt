A released term's broadsheet is the cards that went home

Issue #55. `broadsheet()` promised this in its own docstring — "there is no
frozen snapshot yet — that is task 3 — and once there is, a *released* term must
be served from it rather than from here" — and task 3 shipped in #44 without the
switch being made. `docs/positions.md` and `docs/sessions.md` carried the same
"once task 3 lands", so three places recorded a condition that had been true for
weeks.

## The cause is the roster, not a revision

The issue was first framed as a revision re-ranking only its own card. That is a
consequence. Marks lock at release — `gradebook`'s `0002` trigger refuses an
INSERT, UPDATE or DELETE of a `Score` for a released term — and **nothing
refuses a placement**. So a child placed into a released term (#31) or
transferred out of one moved the live roster under a frozen ranking, and this
page ranked whoever was on the roster now against marks frozen then. Two
rankings for one class, with nothing in the data saying which was current.

`test_a_placement_made_after_release_is_not_on_the_page` asserts both halves:
the trigger's refusal, and the roster moving anyway.

## Every number on the released page comes from the rows on that page

A released sheet is served from `cards.cards_on(sheet)` — one card per child,
highest version, the `DISTINCT ON` that function was kept for and had no caller
for. `current_rank` is `dense_positions()` over the cards' frozen `own_average`
values; `class_average` is `mean_percentage()` over the same values, still never
stored. Columns come from the cards' own subject lines in their frozen print
order, so a subject retired since release still appears on a page released with
it. Nothing here writes, and an unreleased term still reads live marks — a
teacher who could not see their own marking would be reading a stale sheet.

`positions._mean()` becomes public as `mean_percentage()` for that class
average: computing the mean anywhere else would be the second rounding policy
the function was extracted to end.

## The ranks are derived, not read off the cards

`ReleasedCard.position` records where a child came **at that card's own freeze**,
and a revision re-freezes one card against the class as it reads at that later
moment. Reading those numbers off the cards can therefore put two children at
rank 1 on one page — `TheRevisedChildTests.test_the_page_never_repeats_a_rank`
asserts the frozen pair is literally `[1, 1]` where the derived one is `[1, 2]`.

## The fields are renamed, which is a breaking API change

`position` becomes `current_rank` and the per-subject `position` becomes
`current_subject_rank`; `from_snapshot` is new and says which of the two
questions the page answered. `results/card_api.py:34` already names the trap
this closes: `position` meant *rank in the class*, *rank in a subject* and
*where the line prints* in one codebase, and a broadsheet field called
`position` holding a rank was one paste away from being read as print order.
Nothing in the repository consumed the old names.

Row order still differs between the two pages — the live one is in roster order,
the released one comes back ordered by `student_membership_id` because
`cards_on()`'s `DISTINCT ON` requires that leading sort. Both are arbitrary to a
school, which is issue #24, and choosing an order here would pre-empt it.
